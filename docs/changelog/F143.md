# F143: Eden phase F, education and the flow of knowledge

`learning.ts` (state on `Person.learn` and `World.lore`, saved with the world). **Teaching:** a young person who wants a skill attaches to the best-reputed master near them (a master takes at most three pupils; two make a "school"); a master's lesson is worth about two and a half chance lessons, a curious young pupil learns faster, and every lesson costs the teacher energy and hunger. A pupil who reaches the standard is tested and initiated (chronicled); one who is not is told so and counts it as a failure. Reputation for knowing rises with pupils' success and drifts toward true skill. **Oral tradition:** a person who has lived through danger, want or sickness may make a story, song, proverb or myth that carries a rule (keep the fire at night, share in winter, wash before eating). Each retelling can change the telling or lose the rule: a song loses the rule least (3% a telling), a story most (22%); hearing it again restores a lost rule. Whoever still holds the rule leans toward it, always yielding to thirst and hunger. **Access:** crafts can be closed by sex or class, reading can be closed to a class, a guild can keep its craft secret (casual teaching refused, its own apprentices admitted), a book can be banned (taken off the shelf, not readable or copyable), letters are taught only by the lettered, scribes copy books (a slip may drop what a copy teaches), a collapse makes people forget and burns libraries, and strangers carry ideas in and out. **Failure and belief:** a failed act is remembered and makes the person less keen on it; those who watch learn half as much. A failure may breed an explanation drawn from a small catalogue of folk beliefs, some false. Beliefs spread by talk (trust and confidence), are passed on by teachers along with the skill, hold while untried, and are dropped when tried and found false (a traditionalist needs more evidence), after which that person says so and shakes the belief in those who hold it. **Curiosity and words:** curiosity falls with age (a child about 1.6x, an elder about 0.6x the base), shown as exploring toward unseen things and tallied per age band (`curiosityByAge`). Each child takes its parents' words for eight concepts with a one-in-five chance per word of a sound change, so a line's speech drifts: ten generations on, under 60% of the words match.

**Not done, said plainly.** Restrictions by sex, class and a closed reading are not emergent: they exist only when set (`worldLore(w).restrict`), no culture in the sim sets them yet, and nobody is born into a class. Guilds do form on their own (tradition-minded masters of a craft) but only close a craft sometimes. There is no curriculum and no school building; a "school" is a master with two pupils. Teachers' mistakes are limited to the six folk beliefs in `FOLK`; no belief arises from nothing. Tales carry only three rules and a fixed pool of scenes; they are not linked to the songs and stories in `works`. The drifted words are stored and measured but not yet spoken: speech lines still use plain English. Collapse is a function, not an event that the sim triggers. Travellers carry knowledge only when a stranger talks with someone; trade routes do not exist. Libraries as institutions (keepers, lending, rules) are not built. Not yet seen running in the game.

**Closed by the finish pass (F150).** The restrictions now arise on their own: class passes from parents to children (`assignClass`: 5 inherited, 20 risen and 5 elite in a three-year run), the elite close reading to the rest, a craft whose masters are all one sex closes to the other, priests ban books (12 in three years), and a collapse can lose knowledge held only in heads (a death toll or a plague triggers it). Nothing is hand-set any more except in the tests.
