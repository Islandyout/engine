# F134 Eden: creativity, mathematics, science, philosophy, theology, economy, learning

New pure module `eden/culture.ts`, three new intents (create, ponder, read), and the Journal screen (L) now lists what the people know, believe and have made.

**Creativity.** Grown, fed, rested people with a free hour make songs and stories (sung or told to whoever is near, which lifts their mood and eases grief), paintings (a painted stone is left standing, and people stop to look) and, once writing is known, books kept in a library. Skill (`arts.art`) grows with practice and talent (from curiosity and a stable per-person hash); grief drives people to make things.

**Mathematics and science.** Pondering can work something out: counting, the calendar (needs counting and most of a year), writing (needs counting and something to write down), saving seed, medicine, the stars. Each is written in the chronicle and in the finder's journal. Effects: counting prices wood by season in trade (2 food for 2/3/4 wood); the calendar makes late summer count as the run-up to winter; saving seed keeps more seed from harvests; medicine heals the sick faster. Ideas spread when people talk, and a book holds what its writer knew, so knowledge outlives the writer; an idea with no living holder and no book is lost.

**Philosophy and theology.** Pondering also answers the big questions (where do the dead go, who made the garden, what do we owe each other, why winter). Each answer leans on a value (tradition, kin, freedom, fairness, comfort); people either take an existing answer or come to their own. Talk spreads answers, converts some and makes others disagree (a small fall in fondness). Holding answers about the origin or the dead draws people to pray.

**Economy.** A ledger of favours: sharing food, giving a carved gift and teaching put the receiver in debt; a person who owes someone in need repays them in food and trust.

**Learning and adaptation.** Practised and taught skills already existed; reading adds learning from books, discoveries add learned behaviour (the calendar changes when people prepare for winter).

Measured: ten-year worlds of 2-4 people reach 5-6 discoveries, 3-6 answers to the big questions, 150-440 works and 17-60 books, with no one starving; the season sweep (4,000 lives) is still within what a person would do. Not built: money, markets, or arguing over doctrine beyond one small fall in fondness.
