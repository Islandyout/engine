# Eden: what is still missing (audit, 2026-10-30)

An honest list of what the people do not yet have, set against the aim: a human life, good and bad, from infancy to old age, with the
conflicts and reasons behind what people do. Checked against the code (`apps/editor/src/eden/*`), not against what was intended. Where a
thing is "partial", what exists is named so the gap is clear.

**What a person is today.** Five needs (hunger, thirst, energy, social, joy), six emotions (joy, fear, anger, gratitude, pride, shame),
a mood, a personality of a few numbers, five values (kin, fairness, freedom, tradition, comfort), skills that improve with practice,
a memory of places, a relation (affinity, trust, familiarity) to each person, goals, and a choice made by scoring options with a little
noise. Age matters only through a few thresholds (under 1, under 4, under 6, under 14, adult, over 55). Everything below is measured against that.

## 1. The mind itself (the core gap)

The mind is a **utility scorer with hand-set weights**, not a psyche. Missing, in order of how much they change everything else:

1. **No drives beyond five needs.** Missing: safety and security, belonging, esteem and status, autonomy, competence, curiosity/novelty,
   meaning and purpose, sex and attachment as drives, play as a developmental need, rest of the mind (boredom is only a `joy` need).
2. **No neurochemistry.** No simulated dopamine (wanting, reward prediction, craving), serotonin (status, mood stability), oxytocin
   (bonding, trust), cortisol (stress, its build-up and its damage), endorphins (pain relief, runner's high, laughter), noradrenaline
   (arousal, vigilance), testosterone and oestrogen (puberty, drive, aggression, pregnancy), melatonin (sleep rhythm). No receptor
   sensitivity, tolerance, withdrawal or addiction, no chronic stress, no depression or anxiety as states that persist and bias everything.
3. **Only six emotions, no blends.** Missing: love as its own state (today only `affinity`), grief as more than an emotion decaying
   (it exists as `grief`), loneliness, jealousy, envy, guilt, disgust, contempt, hope, despair, awe, surprise, nostalgia, longing, boredom,
   regret, relief, humour as an emotion, empathy.
4. **No theory of mind.** A person does not model what others know, want, believe or feel; gossip passes facts but nobody reasons "she
   thinks I took it". So no real lying, deception, bluffing, flattery, persuasion by interest, secrets, reputation management, surprise
   gifts, or being fooled.
5. **No inner conflict.** Choice is "best score plus noise". Missing: competing motives held at once (duty vs desire, fear vs pride,
   loyalty vs fairness), temptation and self-control, rationalisation, guilt after acting against one's values, ambivalence, addiction
   to habit, acting against one's own interest, double standards, hypocrisy. The duality of people is absent: nobody is kind to one and cruel
   to another for reasons.
6. **No unconscious or habit layer.** No learned habits that run without deciding, no traumas that trigger, no conditioned fears or
   likes, no biases, no moods that colour memory.
7. **Memory is a journal and a map.** No episodic recall that a cue brings back, no forgetting curve, no memory distortion, no
   emotional tagging that makes some memories vivid, no dreams, no sleep consolidation, no storytelling from one's own life, no sense of
   a life story that changes with age (a `story` string is written once at birth).
8. **No self.** No self-image, self-esteem, identity, ideals ("who I want to be"), or sense of fairness about oneself; no reflection
   on one's own choices beyond fixed "lessons".

Consciousness cannot be claimed or measured here. What can be built are the functional pieces above (wants, experience, memory,
a self-model, emotion, norms) so that behaviour shows the same tensions. The aim in this document is behaviour that follows from reasons,
not a claim of experience.

## 2. Life stages (almost entirely missing)

A baby, a child, a youth, an adult and an elder differ only in size, a few allowed actions and death risk. Missing:

- **Infancy (0-2):** attachment to a carer (secure, anxious, avoidant, shaped by how it was cared for), crying and being soothed, feeding
  at the breast and weaning, first words, first steps, stranger fear, object permanence. Today: a baby just exists and is carried.
- **Early childhood (2-6):** language acquisition (a child's vocabulary grows from what is said to it), imitation, play and pretend,
  tantrums, learning rules and being corrected, fear of the dark, imaginary friends, first sense of "mine".
- **Middle childhood (6-12):** apprenticeship by watching and helping, peer groups and play fights, fairness ("it is not fair"), first
  shame, rivalry between siblings, chores, learning stories and customs, first understanding of death.
- **Adolescence (12-19):** puberty (hormones, growth, voice, desire), identity formation, testing limits and rebellion against parents,
  peer status and belonging, first romance and heartbreak, risk-taking, intense friendships, a changing relationship with the family,
  doubting the old beliefs, wanting independence while still depending.
- **Young adulthood (20-35):** choosing a partner and a work, courtship, pair bonding, pregnancy and birth as a risk for both, becoming a
  parent, leaving the parents, ambition.
- **Middle age (35-60):** generativity (passing on, mentoring, building), caring for both children and ageing parents, peak status or its
  loss, midlife doubt, accumulated wealth or debt, the first deaths of peers.
- **Old age (60+):** slowing, illness, loss of strength and sight and memory, dependence on others, being honoured or neglected, telling
  stories as the keeper of knowledge, facing death, wanting to leave something, grief for the many lost. No retirement, no care of the
  elderly as a duty or a burden, no wisdom, no dementia.
- **Sensitive periods and critical windows:** a child who is not spoken to does not learn to speak; one who is not held does not bond. None
  of this exists.
- **Women's and men's lives:** pregnancy exists; missing are breastfeeding, menstrual cycle effects, postpartum states, parenting roles,
  and the many ways labour and status were divided and why.
- **Family structure:** parents and children exist as ids; missing are siblings as a relation, marriage or pairing as a recognised bond
  with obligations, widowhood, remarriage, adoption, orphans' fate, step-families, extended kin and clan, inheritance rules (a simple
  heir exists), incest avoidance.

## 3. Love, pain, joy, meaning

- **Love:** only `affinity` going up. Missing: romantic love with infatuation then attachment, desire, jealousy, fidelity and betrayal,
  heartbreak and its long grief, parental love and its sacrifices, friendship that costs something, unrequited love, love that fails
  or turns to hatred.
- **Pain:** body injuries (a `wound`, illness) exist. Missing: pain as a signal that shapes behaviour, chronic pain, fear of pain, being in
  pain and being unable to help, pain of loss (only a number), shame, humiliation, isolation, abuse, cruelty and its after-effects.
- **Joy:** small joys exist. Missing: joy that comes from meaning, achievement after hardship, belonging, laughter together, celebration,
  festivals and rites of passage, music, dance, play in adults, beauty, generosity.
- **Meaning:** a few discoveries and tenets exist. Missing: a felt purpose that organises a life, crises of meaning, giving one's life
  for something, legacy, vocation, the search for answers when the world is hard, how religion and ritual console, why people bury the dead.

## 4. Violence, cruelty and the bad side (almost entirely missing)

Quarrel, shun, steal and a spear against beasts exist. Missing, and necessary for the "why humans did it":

- Fights between people, injury and killing of people, murder, execution, war between groups, raids, revenge and feuds, bullying, abuse
  of children, rape and its place in history (to be modelled soberly or left out by choice), slavery and exploitation, coercion.
- Greed, hoarding, cheating, fraud, bribery, betrayal, informing, scapegoating, mob behaviour, tribalism, prejudice, propaganda.
- Why: scarcity, fear, status competition, honour, resource guarding, in-group loyalty, desperation, power, and how the same person
  can be tender and cruel.
- Consequences and institutions: shame and honour cultures, law, judges, punishment, exile, forgiveness, reconciliation, restitution,
  prisons, guilt carried for life, vengeance cycles and how they end.

## 5. Economy: why it came about, and money

Today: wood and food carried, a fixed barter ratio that depends on season once counting is known, and a ledger of favours. Missing,
as a chain of causes rather than a feature list:

1. **Ownership and property:** things have no owner (a grave and a dog have `of`). Missing: owning a field, a tool, a house, a catch;
   theft as a crime against someone; borders; inheritance of land.
2. **Scarcity, surplus and specialisation:** roles exist as skill labels but nobody lives off a speciality; no surplus to trade, no
   comparative advantage, no dependence on others' work, no guilds or crafts, no tools that raise output.
3. **Trade beyond swaps:** no trading partners at distance, no caravans or boats, no markets or fairs, no prices that move with supply and
   demand, no haggling, no trust networks, no middlemen, no storage and spoilage that make timing matter.
4. **Money:** no medium of exchange (shells, salt, metal), no store of value, no unit of account, no coinage, no debt as a thing with
   interest, no credit, no ledger beyond favours, no tax or tribute.
5. **Capital, investment and stocks:** no saving to invest, no seed that is not eaten, no tools or land improved for later return, no
   shares in a venture (a boat, a mill), no lending and interest, no insurance (a granary or a clan as insurance), no speculation, no bubbles
   or crashes, no inequality that compounds, no poverty traps, no class.
6. **Work and labour:** no wages, no employment, no slavery, no division between those who work and those who command.
7. **Institutions that make it possible:** property law, contracts, weights and measures, writing for accounts, trust and reputation,
   enforcement. (Counting and writing exist as discoveries; nothing uses them for accounts.)

## 6. Education and the flow of knowledge

Exists: practised skills, an adult shows a child, talk spreads ideas, books keep knowledge, discoveries can be lost. Missing:

- Structured teaching: apprenticeship with a master, initiation, schools, curriculum, tests, reputations for knowing.
- Oral tradition and its distortion: stories that change in the telling, songs as memory aids, proverbs, myths that carry rules.
- Who is allowed to learn what (gender, class, guild secrets), literacy as power, libraries as institutions, censorship, lost knowledge in a
  collapse, knowledge carried by travel and trade, specialist scholars, copying of books.
- Learning from failure and from others' failure, teaching being costly to the teacher, teachers' errors being passed on, false beliefs
  that spread and persist (some of what is "known" should be wrong).
- Curiosity as a driver that is stronger in the young; language itself being learned and changing.

## 7. Society, norms and politics

Exists: norms and customs that fade or strengthen, shunning, roles, a chronicle. Missing: leaders and followers, status hierarchy, councils,
chiefs, inherited rank, laws and judging, taboos, rites of passage, marriage customs, funerals as rituals with meaning, festivals,
feuds between families, alliances, other settlements and trade or war with them, migration, splitting of the group, founder effects,
cultural drift (different groups developing different customs and languages), religion as an institution with priests and conflicts.

## 8. Thinking and problem solving (partly built in F135/F136)

Built: plans from distances and time, experiments and three inventions, a party against a wolf, scouting, a learned cause (fire and wolves),
an optional model that proposes plans. Still missing: general cause-and-effect learning (only one rule exists), analogies, teaching
how to solve rather than what, shared long plans by many, planning over years, risk assessment of unfamiliar things, learning that a
belief was wrong and updating, a large space of things to invent (three exist), tool chains (pot, loom, kiln, bow), agriculture as a
revolution rather than a plot of plants, domestication, metals, wheels, boats and bridges that people use.

## 9. Body and world

- **Body:** no growth curve with real height and strength, no menarche or menopause, no ageing of senses, no disability, no scars or
  injuries that last, no fitness, no sleep debt, no hygiene beyond a number, no pregnancy complications beyond one risk, no sexual health.
- **World:** one garden and its surroundings; no other peoples, no trade routes, no real geography (rivers, mountains, coast), no long
  climate changes, no disease epidemics across settlements, no resource depletion that causes collapse, no fire as a danger to the camp,
  no domestic animals beyond a dog.
- **Presentation (carried over from earlier work):** faces and eye blinks, a real lying-down clip, swimming and rowing (the rowboat is
  decoration), music beyond the generated tune, lighting polish, visible wear and dirt unverified, people's look not editable.

## 10. Things that fell through the cracks in the build itself

- Never seen working in the running game: the rowboat, birds, leaping fish, snow cover, music, belly/dirt/limp visuals, the journal and
  pack screens, the people form, the mic button, the pad buttons, the plan and party behaviour, the model-planner. All pass tests and the
  simulation; headless screenshots did not show them.
- The free model (Ollama) has never been run against this code. Voice, chat, planner and rewording are tested only with fake answers.
- Installers 10-14 showed people in underwear (fixed in 15); every earlier installer should be treated as superseded.
- The old Supabase `eden-tick` function from the first version is still running on the live project; it was left alone on purpose and
  never switched off.
- A stray remote branch `eden-takeover` could not be deleted (refused by the sandbox).
- `docs/EDEN_THINGS.md` lists about 300 objects to build; only small joys and a handful (furniture, hut, shrine, painted stone, library,
  weir, snare, net) exist. Cooking, pots, ovens, boats, bridges as used objects, play objects, farm works and animal companions are not built.
- Eden-specific code has no browser test of its own (the CI browser test covers the editor, not the garden).
- Performance with many people is measured only for the mind (4.6 ms a step at 60 people and 200 animals); rendering many bodies is unmeasured.
- The Creator's own character (the Walker) has no body customisation, no needs, no ageing, no consequences from what they do to people
  beyond trust and a few events.
- Documentation: `docs/IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md` rows exist through F136, but there is no single design document for the mind, and the
  older Supabase-era docs still describe the first version.

## 11. A way to build it (so the pieces fit, not bolt on)

The right order is to change the **core** first, because everything above hangs from it:

1. **A drive and chemistry layer.** Replace five needs with a drive set (safety, belonging, esteem, autonomy, competence, curiosity,
   meaning, attachment, sex) and a small set of slow variables (dopamine, serotonin, oxytocin, cortisol, endorphin, noradrenaline, sex
   hormones, melatonin) with production, decay and tolerance. Choice uses them as weights, so mood, stress and bonding change what
   people want. Measure with the existing life sweep (no regressions to survival).
2. **A life-stage model.** Age bands with their tasks, hormones, abilities (language, theory of mind, abstract thought) and the
   attachment history that shapes the adult.
3. **A self and a theory of mind.** What I think you know and want; my reputation; my self-image. Enables lies, secrets, jealousy,
   betrayal, forgiveness, shame culture.
4. **Ownership, then economy.** Property, then surplus and specialisation, then money, credit and markets, each arising from the one before.
5. **Institutions.** Law, leadership, ritual, schooling, as outcomes of norms plus conflict, not scripted.
6. **A wider world.** Other groups, trade, war, migration.
7. **Violence and its consequences**, once there are institutions to answer it.

Each step needs the same discipline as before: a test that fails on the old code, a long-run sweep that shows nothing regresses, and
a plain account of what is still missing.
