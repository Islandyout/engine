# The City Template: how games build towns that work

The City Template (`games/city-template`, generated by `tools/city/`) is a small working town for a game to start from. It has roads with signalised junctions and zoned blocks. Its streets are furnished by rule. Traffic keeps to its lane and stops on red. People walk the sidewalks and cross with the light, some walking dogs. Wildlife lives in the park.

This page covers three things:
- what it is built from: published techniques, planning guides, and what players say on forums and in reviews;
- every rule and number the generator uses;
- how to build a game on it.

## What makes a city feel alive: what players say

We read human-written discussion: GTAForums, Steam and GameFAQs boards, ResetEra, mod pages and reviews. The same points come up again and again.

- **Variety beats density.** A GTAForums poster said the crowd's behaviour is what makes a city feel alive, not its size. Players complain about the same three or four cars spawning together, and about clone pedestrians. The SCS forum asks for people with activity hours, not the same NPCs doing the same thing at every hour.
  - **Template:** traffic is drawn from eight car models, and no two cars in a row are the same. People cycle through eleven models.
- **Empty reads as dead.** Cyberpunk 2077's launch "ghost town" is the standing example (Steam threads, Game Rant). One player wrote that a small town in Red Dead Redemption 2 is more alive than the entire city.
  - **Template:** every block side has people on it, and every block has cars around it. The park has its own life.
- **Traffic should obey the rules, and it's noticed when it doesn't.** Cities: Skylines II players flagged cars ignoring banned turns and pedestrians jaywalking anywhere, which jams roads (PCGamesN, Steam). GTA modders keep adding longer light cycles so queues build, plus working crosswalk signals.
  - **Template:** cars stop at the line on red. People cross only at crosswalks, on the walk light.
- **AI should react sensibly.** GTA IV's pedestrians are remembered for walking away when bumped, where GTA V's start fights. Drivers that plough straight into you top the GTA V complaints.
  - **Template:** traffic brakes for anyone on foot in its path. People on foot never attack. Animals flee rather than fight.
- **Routines sell it.** Players praise RDR2's townsfolk going to work and the saloon. A GameFAQs thread caught shopkeepers standing in shops that were closed.
  - **Template:** the engine's Routine component (daily stops on the scene clock) is there for games to add schedules. The Walker routes are the always-on base layer.
- **No pop-in in front of the player.** Respawning traffic should happen out of sight. GTA does it behind the camera, and players notice when it doesn't.
  - **Template:** all its people and cars are placed at load; nothing pops in.
- **Optional strictness.** Players disagree about traffic law enforcement. The Mafia remake made police strictness a slider; Driver's instant red-light chases were remembered as harsh.
  - **Template:** law is left to the game. Traffic obeys the rules; the player isn't policed.

## How cities are built in games: the techniques

- **A road graph first, then blocks, lots and buildings.** This is the standard pipeline from Parish and Müller's procedural cities (SIGGRAPH 2001). Street patterns can come from L-systems or tensor fields (Chen et al., 2008; the open-source MapGenerator). The template uses a regular grid, the simplest case of the same pipeline, and keeps the stages separate so a game can swap the grid for its own graph.
- **Lanes as data.** Traffic follows lanes, not whole roads:
  - GTA's path nodes give vehicles and peds separate graphs;
  - CARLA (OpenDRIVE) has lanes, sidewalks, junctions and signals, with a Traffic Manager driving NPCs;
  - Cities: Skylines connects lanes through junctions.
  - In the template, every car follows the right-hand lane line, and every walker the sidewalk's through zone.
- **Signals belong to the junction.** Cars decide for themselves whether to stop. The template's `TrafficSignal` owns the cycle. Each driver checks the line ahead, and each walker checks the light for its crossing.
- **Perimeter blocks.** Buildings front the street at the sidewalk's edge, and the courtyard behind takes parking or gardens. This is how most real downtowns and older European blocks work.
- **Street furniture by zones** (the NACTO *Urban Street Design Guide*): the sidewalk divides into a frontage zone by the buildings, a clear through zone for walking (1.5–2.4 m residential, 2.4–3.6 m downtown) and a furniture zone at the kerb (trees, lamps, hydrants, benches, bins, bus stops).
- **Foliage by rule, not by hand** (procedural scatter, as engines' foliage tools do):
  - noise gathers plants into groves with clearings;
  - minimum spacing (Poisson-disk) stops overlaps;
  - slope, altitude and ground-layer rules decide where each species grows;
  - every instance gets its own yaw, scale and tint;
  - small plants lean with the ground, trees grow straight up.
  - Draw cost is kept down with instancing, chunk culling and a far LOD. All of these are in the engine (see `foliage.ts` and `terrainMesh.ts`).

## The rules and numbers the template uses

| Rule | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Road spacing (centre to centre) | 96 m (80 m blocks) | `GRID` |
| Carriageway | 2 lanes × 4 m; right-hand traffic, lane line 2 m off centre | kit tile, `LANE` |
| Sidewalk | 4 m: furniture zone at 0.8 m from the kerb, people walk at 2.4 m | `FURNITURE`, `THROUGH` |
| Stop line | 7 m from the junction centre (before the crosswalk) | `STOP`, `TrafficSignal.radius` |
| Signal cycle | 14 s green, 3 s yellow, 2 s all red per road (38 s) | `TrafficSignal` |
| Green wave | offset = travel time between junctions at ~11 m/s | `buildCity` |
| Stopping | planned at 3 m/s² with a metre to spare; a car too close to stop on yellow goes on (the dilemma-zone rule) | `street.hpp` |
| Walk light | step off only on your road's green, if green + yellow + all red cover the crossing at walking speed | `may_cross` |
| Unsignalised crossing | wait while a moving car is within 24 m, or any car within 4 m | `safe_to_cross` |
| Street trees | every 10 m in the furniture zone; none within 10 m of a junction (sight triangle), 4 m of a hydrant or 6 m of a bus stop; none on industrial streets | `streetSide` |
| Lamps | midway between every third pair of trees | `streetSide` |
| Hydrant | one per block side, ~10 m from the corner | `streetSide` |
| Bus stops | on downtown and mixed streets, past the first junction, with a bin | `streetSide` |
| Parking | courtyard lots only: 2.6 × 5.5 m stalls on a 6 m aisle, ~70% full; never in a traffic lane | `buildCity` |
| Building setback | 0.5 m commercial, 4 m residential (front gardens) | `buildCity` |
| Zoning | downtown (towers, offices) centre; mixed (shops, apartments) round it; residential outside; industrial (warehouses, garages) on an edge; one park | `defaultZones` |
| Park | groves (cluster 0.7–0.8, 11 m spacing), bushes in clumps, two paths, benches | foliage rules |
| People | 1.2–1.55 m/s; loop the outer sidewalks of 1–2 × 1–2 blocks, crossing side streets at the junctions | `Walker` |
| Dogs | follow their owner 1.4 m behind | `Walker.follow` |
| Wildlife | foxes and a deer in the park: wary at 18 m, flee at 8 m, stay within 30 m | `Wildlife` |

## Building a game on it

- Regenerate with `cd apps/editor && npm run city` (writes `examples/city/city-template.json`), or open **City Template** from the editor's Games library.
- `buildCity(scene, options)` in `tools/city/city.ts` adds the town to any `SceneBuilder`:
  - options: blocks, seed, zones, numbers of cars, people and dogs;
  - it returns the road lines, junctions, blocks and a spawn point for placing your own content.
- The parts are separate engine features you can use anywhere:
  - `TrafficSignal` and `Walker` (components);
  - the foliage options in any Terrain `scatter`;
  - `trunk` collisions in `ModelInstances`;
  - the realistic vehicles, people, animals and trees in the catalog.

## Not yet

- No time-of-day density, rush hour or night-time thinning. A game script can do this with `world.spawn` and the scene clock.
- No lane changes, left-turn phases or pedestrian countdowns. Traffic turns from the right lane.
- No on-street parking. The kit's tiles have no parking lane, so parked cars live in courtyard lots.
- No navmesh for pedestrians. Walkers follow authored routes, and Routine NPCs use the nav grid.
- There is only one tree style per species and no seasonal variation.
